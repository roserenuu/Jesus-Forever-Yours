Bible verses for the New Year

A new year is a natural moment to look back with gratitude and look ahead with hope. Many people set goals, choose a word for the year, or simply want a fresh start. These verses offer a faith-filled way to begin, with promises about God's guidance, his care through every season, and the new things he is able to do.

Psalm 65:11Revelation 21:5Isaiah 58:11James 4:15Ezekiel 36:26

You crown the year with a bountiful harvest; even the hard pathways overflow with abundance.

Psalm 65:11

Psalm 65 is a harvest song that celebrates God's generosity in creation, rain on the fields, grain in abundance, and hills dressed with joy. The picture of God crowning the year is a lovely one for New Year's Day. It also includes an honest phrase, even the hard pathways overflow. The psalmist does not pretend every road was easy, but he finds God's goodness along those roads too.

ReflectLooking back on the past year, where did you see God's goodness, including on a harder pathway?

And the one sitting on the throne said, “Look, I am making everything new!” And then he said to me, “Write this down, for what I tell you is trustworthy and true.”

Revelation 21:5

These words come near the very end of the Bible, in John's vision of a new heaven and a new earth. God himself is speaking, and he describes his work in the present tense, I am making everything new. John is told to write it down because it is trustworthy and true. A new year is a small echo of that promise, a reminder that renewal is something God loves to do.

ReflectWhat area of your life would you like to invite God to make new this year?

The Lord will guide you continually, giving you water when you are dry and restoring your strength. You will be like a well-watered garden, like an ever-flowing spring.

Isaiah 58:11

This promise comes at the end of a passage about generous living, sharing food with the hungry and caring for those in need. God promises continual guidance, refreshment when we feel dry, and renewed strength. The picture of a well-watered garden and a spring that never runs out is a beautiful image to carry into a new year, a life that is both nourished and able to nourish others.

ReflectWhere would you like God's guidance or refreshment most in the year ahead?

What you ought to say is, “If the Lord wants us to, we will live and do this or that.”

James 4:15

James is writing to people making confident plans for business trips and profits. He reminds them that life is uncertain and suggests a humble phrase, if the Lord wants us to. This is not a call to stop setting goals. It is a gentle invitation to make plans prayerfully, holding them with open hands. It is a helpful posture for anyone writing out resolutions for the year ahead.

ReflectHow could you write your goals for this year in a way that keeps God at the center of your plans?

And I will give you a new heart, and I will put a new spirit in you. I will take out your stony, stubborn heart and give you a tender, responsive heart.

Ezekiel 36:26

God spoke this promise through Ezekiel to people in exile who longed for a fresh start. The change he describes goes deeper than better habits. He promises a new heart and a new spirit, replacing what is stony and stubborn with something tender and responsive. Resolutions often focus on outward change, and this verse points to the inner renewal that only God can give.

ReflectWhat would it look like for your heart to become a little more tender and responsive to God this year?
A closer look

Going a little deeper.

The Bible marks time with many fresh starts. God gave Israel a calendar of feasts and new beginnings, including the start of the year in the spring and the Day of Atonement in the fall. Lamentations speaks of mercies that begin fresh each morning. Jesus began his public ministry with a call to turn toward God's Kingdom. Throughout Scripture, new beginnings are not about pretending the past did not happen. They are about receiving God's grace and walking forward with him.

A simple way to begin the year is to set aside a quiet hour on New Year's Day or the first weekend of January. Read through these verses slowly, write down a few things you are thankful for from the past year, and then list a handful of hopes for the year ahead. Some people choose one verse as a theme for the whole year and return to it each month. Others share their goals with a friend and pray for one another along the way.

Ways to keep these verses close. Choose one verse to read each morning this week and notice how it shapes your day. Write a favorite verse on a card for your desk, mirror, or nightstand. Share one verse with a friend or family member who might enjoy it, along with a short personal note.

A prayer

Lord, thank you for your faithfulness through the past year, on the easy roads and the hard ones. As this new year begins, I invite you to make things new in me. Guide me continually, refresh me when I feel dry, and give me a tender heart toward you and others. I offer my plans to you, and I trust you with every season ahead. Amen.
