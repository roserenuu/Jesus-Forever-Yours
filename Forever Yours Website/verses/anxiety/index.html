Bible verses for Anxiety

Scripture has a lot to say about worry, and most of it is an invitation. These verses offer a steady place to rest your thoughts, whether you are carrying a heavy week or simply want a calmer start to the day.

Philippians 4:6-71 Peter 5:7Matthew 6:34Isaiah 41:10Psalm 94:19John 14:27Psalm 55:22Matthew 11:28

Don’t worry about anything; instead, pray about everything. Tell God what you need, and thank him for all he has done. Then you will experience God’s peace, which exceeds anything we can understand. His peace will guard your hearts and minds as you live in Christ Jesus.

Philippians 4:6-7

Paul wrote these words from a prison cell, which makes his calm all the more striking. He does not ask readers to ignore their concerns but to turn each one into a prayer, paired with thanks for what God has already done. The promise that follows is peace that stands guard like a sentry over both heart and mind. It is a pattern worth practicing daily, one request and one thank you at a time.

ReflectConsider naming one specific request and one specific thank you to God today, and notice how pairing them changes the way the request feels.

Give all your worries and cares to God, for he cares about you.

1 Peter 5:7

The word give here pictures throwing a load onto someone else's shoulders. Peter grounds the invitation in a simple reason, God cares about you. Worries are not too small or too large to hand over, because the One receiving them is personally interested in the one carrying them. That care is the foundation, and handing over our worries is the response.

ReflectWhat is one concern you could intentionally hand to God this week, picturing it lifted from your shoulders and placed into the care of someone who loves you?

“So don’t worry about tomorrow, for tomorrow will bring its own worries. Today’s trouble is enough for today.

Matthew 6:34

Jesus speaks here with gentle realism. He does not promise that tomorrow will be free of challenges, only that each day comes with its own measure of grace. Living one day at a time is not avoidance but trust, a choice to meet today with full attention and leave tomorrow in the Father's hands.

ReflectWhat would it look like to give full attention to today's tasks and relationships, trusting God with tomorrow's plans when tomorrow arrives?

Don’t be afraid, for I am with you. Don’t be discouraged, for I am your God. I will strengthen you and help you. I will hold you up with my victorious right hand.

Isaiah 41:10

This verse stacks one promise on another. God is present, God is yours, God will strengthen, help, and hold you up. The image of a victorious right hand comes from the ancient picture of a king's strong arm, the hand that protects and leads. It is a verse many people memorize because every line offers something steady to stand on.

ReflectWhich of the five promises in this verse speaks most clearly to your day, presence, belonging, strength, help, or being held up?

When doubts filled my mind, your comfort gave me renewed hope and cheer.

Psalm 94:19

The psalmist is honest that doubts can crowd the mind. What changes the scene is not the absence of questions but the arrival of God's comfort. The result is renewed hope and even cheer, a reminder that God meets us in the middle of our thinking and brings light into it.

ReflectThink of a time when God's comfort brought fresh hope into a crowded mind, and let that memory encourage you as you read this verse again.

“I am leaving you with a gift—peace of mind and heart. And the peace I give is a gift the world cannot give. So don’t be troubled or afraid.

John 14:27

Jesus spoke these words to his friends on the night before the cross, a time when they had every reason to be unsettled. He describes his peace as a gift, something given rather than earned, and different in kind from the calm the world offers. It does not depend on circumstances, which is why it can hold steady when circumstances shift.

ReflectHow is the peace Jesus describes different from the temporary calm the world offers, and where could you make room to receive it today?

Give your burdens to the Lord, and he will take care of you. He will not permit the godly to slip and fall.

Psalm 55:22

David pictures burdens as something that can be set down and placed in God's care. The promise is twofold, God will take care of you, and he will keep the godly from slipping. It is an invitation to stop carrying alone what was never meant to be carried alone, and to walk with surer footing as a result.

ReflectPicture setting a heavy bag down at someone's feet. What might you set down before the Lord as you pray this verse?

Then Jesus said, “Come to me, all of you who are weary and carry heavy burdens, and I will give you rest.

Matthew 11:28

This is one of the warmest invitations in all of Scripture. Jesus addresses everyone who is weary, without conditions or qualifications, and offers rest as a gift from himself. The rest he describes goes deeper than a good night's sleep. It is the settled rest of the soul that comes from being near him.

ReflectWhere in your week could you build a small moment of rest with Jesus, perhaps a quiet walk, a pause before a meal, or a few minutes of prayer?
A closer look

Going a little deeper.

Notice how often these passages pair an instruction with a reason. Do not worry, because God cares. Do not be afraid, because he is with you. Scripture rarely asks us to simply stop feeling something. Instead it gives us something truer to think about. A helpful practice is to choose one verse from this page and pray it slowly in your own words, naming the specific things on your mind and then naming one thing you are thankful for. Over time this rhythm of honest prayer and gratitude, which Paul describes in Philippians, becomes a familiar path back to peace.

Ways to keep these verses close. Choose one verse to read each morning this week and notice how it shapes your day. Write a favorite verse on a card for your desk, mirror, or nightstand. Share one verse with a friend or family member who might enjoy it, along with a short personal note.

A prayer

Lord, I bring you every thought that feels too big to hold. Teach me to pray about everything and to trust you with what I cannot control. Let your peace guard my heart and mind today. Amen.
