A closer look

Going a little deeper.

Children learn best through repetition, rhythm, and real life. A family might pick one verse a week, read it at breakfast, say it together in the car, and talk about it at bedtime. Writing a verse on a sticky note for a bathroom mirror or adding it to a drawing can help it stick. Some families set a verse to a simple tune, which is how many people still remember Scripture they learned decades ago.

It also helps to connect verses to everyday moments. When a child shares a toy, a parent can mention a verse about kindness. When a child is nervous before a big day, a parent can remind them that Jesus welcomes them and is with them. Teachers can open a lesson with one short verse from this page. Kids learn what a verse means by watching the adults around them live it out with warmth and patience.

Ways to keep these verses close. Choose one verse to say together each morning this week. Write a favorite on a card for a lunchbox, mirror, or nightstand. Share one with a friend, a class, or a grandchild, along with a short personal note.

A prayer for kids

Dear God, thank you for loving me and calling me your child. Thank you that Jesus is always glad when I come to him. Help me grow in wisdom, in kindness, and in love for you and for others. Help me remember your words in my heart, and help me listen well to the people who care for me. In Jesus' name, amen.

Questions

A few common questions.

What are good Bible verses for kids to memorize?

Short verses stick. Psalm 56:3, Psalm 23:1, Genesis 1:1, Philippians 4:13, and Matthew 19:14 are easy to say aloud at bedtime or in the car. This page groups more short NLT verses by theme.

What Bible verse says let the children come to me?

Matthew 19:14 in the New Living Translation reads, But Jesus said, Let the children come to me. Don't stop them! For the Kingdom of Heaven belongs to those who are like these children.

How can parents and teachers help children learn Bible verses?

Repeat one verse for a week at breakfast, in the car, and at bedtime. Write it on a card. Set it to a simple tune. Deuteronomy 6:6 to 7 describes faith talked about at home, on the road, going to bed, and getting up.

What is a good Bible verse for children who are afraid?

Psalm 56:3 is short enough for a child to keep: But when I am afraid, I will put my trust in you. Joshua 1:9 and Isaiah 41:10 also remind kids that God is with them.

Which Bible translation is used here?

Every verse on this page is quoted from the New Living Translation. The Jesus Forever Yours app also includes the NLT and KJV, with more translations in Pro.
