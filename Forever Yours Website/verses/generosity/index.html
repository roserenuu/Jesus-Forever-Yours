Bible verses About Generosity and Giving

Generosity shows up all through Scripture, from farmers sharing their harvest to the early church caring for one another. These verses speak about giving money, time, and kindness, and they describe giving as a source of joy rather than a burden. They are a good companion for year end giving, Giving Tuesday, a church stewardship series, or simply a desire to live with open hands.

Proverbs 11:25Acts 20:35Luke 6:382 Corinthians 9:61 Timothy 6:17-18

The generous will prosper; those who refresh others will themselves be refreshed.

Proverbs 11:25

This proverb describes a kind of exchange that runs deeper than money. People who refresh others often find their own spirits lifted in the process. The word prosper here points to a full and flourishing life, not a guarantee of wealth. Generous people tend to live with a lightness that tight fists rarely know.

ReflectWho could you refresh this week with a gift, a meal, or an encouraging word?

And I have been a constant example of how you can help those in need by working hard. You should remember the words of the Lord Jesus: ‘It is more blessed to give than to receive.’

Acts 20:35

Paul spoke these words while saying goodbye to church leaders in Ephesus, people he had worked alongside for years. He reminded them that he had supported himself so he could help others. Then he quoted a saying of Jesus that does not appear in the Gospels, preserved here so it would not be lost. It is more blessed to give than to receive, a truth many people discover the first time they give freely.

ReflectWhen have you experienced the blessing of giving more than receiving?

Give, and you will receive. Your gift will return to you in full—pressed down, shaken together to make room for more, running over, and poured into your lap. The amount you give will determine the amount you get back.

Luke 6:38

Jesus paints a picture from the marketplace, where grain was poured into a measure, pressed down, and shaken to fill every gap. God's response to generosity is pictured as overflowing, spilling into the giver's lap. The verse is less about a formula for getting rich and more about the generous character of God. People who give freely tend to experience that generosity coming back in many forms.

ReflectWhat would it look like to give with the same overflowing generosity God shows you?

Remember this—a farmer who plants only a few seeds will get a small crop. But the one who plants generously will get a generous crop.

2 Corinthians 9:6

Paul wrote this while gathering a gift for struggling believers in Jerusalem. He used a farming image every reader would understand. A farmer who holds back seed to keep it safe ends up with a thin harvest. Giving is like planting, an act of trust that something good will grow.

ReflectWhat seed of generosity could you plant this month, even if it feels small?

Teach those who are rich in this world not to be proud and not to trust in their money, which is so unreliable. Their trust should be in God, who richly gives us all we need for our enjoyment. Tell them to use their money to do good. They should be rich in good works and generous to those in need, always being ready to share with others.

1 Timothy 6:17-18

Paul gave Timothy a message for wealthy members of his church, and it fits anyone with more than they need. Money is called unreliable, while God is described as the one who richly gives us all we need for our enjoyment. Enjoyment and generosity sit side by side here. The call is to be rich in good works, ready to share whenever the chance comes.

ReflectWhere could you be rich in good works this week, beyond giving money?
A closer look

Going a little deeper.

Searches for verses about giving rise every year around Thanksgiving, Giving Tuesday, and the end of the year, when many people decide where to give. These passages can guide that decision. Some families choose one cause to support together and read Proverbs 11:25 before writing the check. Others set aside a jar for spontaneous giving through December. Churches often use 2 Corinthians 9:6 and Luke 6:38 during a stewardship Sunday.

Generosity is not only about money. Time, attention, hospitality, skills, and encouragement are all gifts the Bible values. A retired teacher who tutors a neighbor, a busy parent who brings a meal to a new family, and a teenager who shovels a driveway are all practicing what these verses describe. Starting small and giving cheerfully matters more than the size of the gift.

Ways to keep these verses close. Choose one verse to read each morning this week and notice how it shapes your day. Write a favorite verse on a card for your desk, mirror, or nightstand. Share one verse with a friend or family member who might enjoy it, along with a short personal note.

A prayer

Generous Father, everything I have comes from your hand. Thank you for giving so freely, most of all in sending Jesus. Open my eyes to the needs around me and open my hands to meet them. Free me from trusting in money, and fill me with joy in sharing my time, my resources, and my kindness. Make me a person who refreshes others. Amen.
