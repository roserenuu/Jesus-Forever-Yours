Psalm 91 Prayer for Protection

Psalm 91 is one of the most searched passages in the Bible, and it is easy to see why. It is a song of trust in God's shelter and care. Here is the psalm in the New Living Translation, followed by a prayer drawn from its words.

Psalm 91:1-4Psalm 91:9-12Psalm 91:14-16

Those who live in the shelter of the Most High will find rest in the shadow of the Almighty. This I declare about the Lord: He alone is my refuge, my place of safety; he is my God, and I trust him. For he will rescue you from every trap and protect you from deadly disease. He will cover you with his feathers. He will shelter you with his wings. His faithful promises are your armor and protection.

Psalm 91:1-4

The opening of Psalm 91 moves from shelter, to declaration, to promise. The image of being covered with feathers and sheltered under wings recalls a mother bird gathering her young. The psalm calls God's faithful promises armor, a protection that is both gentle and strong. Reading these verses aloud has long been a way to begin the day with trust.

ReflectTry reading these verses aloud, replacing you with your own name or the name of someone you love, and notice how personal the promises become. Which image, shelter, shadow, feathers, or armor, stays with you most?

If you make the Lord your refuge, if you make the Most High your shelter, no evil will conquer you; no plague will come near your home. For he will order his angels to protect you wherever you go. They will hold you up with their hands so you won’t even hurt your foot on a stone.

Psalm 91:9-12

Here the psalm describes what it means to make the Lord your refuge and shelter. The famous image of angels holding someone up so they won't stub a foot on a stone shows care down to the smallest detail. This is the passage quoted during Jesus's temptation in the wilderness, and he answered by trusting the Father rather than testing him. It is a reminder that God's protection invites trust, not presumption.

ReflectWhat is the difference between trusting God and testing him, as Jesus showed in the wilderness? Consider how that distinction might shape the way you pray these verses. Where would you like to sense God's care in the small details of your day?

The Lord says, “I will rescue those who love me. I will protect those who trust in my name. When they call on me, I will answer; I will be with them in trouble. I will rescue and honor them. I will reward them with a long life and give them my salvation.”

Psalm 91:14-16

The psalm ends with God himself speaking. Each line is a personal promise, rescue, protection, answer, presence, honor, and salvation. The relationship at the center is love and trust, those who love God and trust his name. It is a beautiful closing that turns a psalm about protection into a psalm about belonging.

ReflectRead God's promises in these verses one at a time, rescue, protection, answer, presence, honor, salvation. Which one feels most meaningful to you today? Consider thanking God specifically for that promise in prayer.
A closer look

Going a little deeper.

Psalm 91 has been read in homes, hospitals, and on journeys for thousands of years. Its structure is worth noticing. It begins with a declaration of trust, moves into promises spoken to the reader, and ends with God speaking in the first person. In other words, the psalm becomes a conversation. Many people pray it by inserting their own name or the names of loved ones into the promises. Others read it aloud each morning or evening for a season. However you use it, the heart of the psalm is relationship, the security that comes from living close to the Most High.

Scholars note that Psalm 91 has no named author in its heading, which may be part of why it has felt so universal to readers across generations. Its promises are not a guarantee that nothing difficult will ever happen. Jesus himself showed in the wilderness that the psalm is not a formula for testing God. Instead it is an invitation to trust. The psalm speaks of God's presence in every circumstance, at night and at noon, at home and on the road. Many families have a tradition of reading it together at the start of a new year, before a move, or before sending a child off to school or college.

Ways to keep these verses close. Choose one verse to read each morning this week and notice how it shapes your day. Write a favorite verse on a card for your desk, mirror, or nightstand. Share one verse with a friend or family member who might enjoy it, along with a short personal note.

A prayer

Most High God, I choose to live in your shelter and rest in your shadow. You are my refuge and my place of safety. Cover me with your faithful promises today. Amen.
