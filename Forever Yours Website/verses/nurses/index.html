Bible verses for nurses: comfort, strength, and a prayer

Nurses, doctors, aides, therapists, and every member of a care team spend their days looking after people at vulnerable moments. These New Living Translation verses honor the calling of care, speak to long shifts, and make a thoughtful gift for a nursing graduate, a coworker, or anyone in healthcare.

A closer look

Five verses up close.

Start here if you want a few passages with room to sit with them. Below, more NLT verses are grouped for the calling of care, long shifts, and tired hearts.

"And the King will say, 'I tell you the truth, when you did it to one of the least of these my brothers and sisters, you were doing it to me!'

Matthew 25:40 · NLT

Jesus tells a story about the final judgment in which the King welcomes people who fed the hungry, clothed the needy, and visited the sick. They are surprised, because they do not remember serving the King at all. His answer gives everyday care a remarkable dignity. Every patient who is turned, comforted, or listened to is someone Jesus takes personally. Care given to them is care given to him.

ReflectHow might seeing each patient or person you serve as someone Jesus cares about change the way you approach a routine task?

"Then a despised Samaritan came along, and when he saw the man, he felt compassion for him. Going over to him, the Samaritan soothed his wounds with olive oil and wine and bandaged them. Then he put the man on his own donkey and took him to an inn, where he took care of him.

Luke 10:33 to 34 · NLT

Jesus told the story of the Good Samaritan to answer the question, who is my neighbor. Notice how practical the Samaritan's compassion is. He cleans the wounds, applies what medicine he has, bandages them, provides transport, and arranges ongoing care at the inn. It reads almost like an early picture of first aid and later care. Compassion in this story is not only a feeling. It is skilled, practical help.

ReflectWhich practical act of compassion in this story most reflects the care you give or have received?

The Lord nurses them when they are sick and restores them to health.

Psalm 41:3 · NLT

This line from Psalm 41 uses a striking image. The Lord himself nurses the sick and restores them to health. Healthcare workers can find both comfort and meaning here. Their work reflects something God does, and they are never the only ones caring for a patient. Medicine, skill, and compassion all work within the larger care of a God who sees each person in the bed.

ReflectHow does it feel to know that God is also caring for the people you look after?

For God is not unjust. He will not forget how hard you have worked for him and how you have shown your love to him by caring for other believers, as you still do.

Hebrews 6:10 · NLT

The writer of Hebrews reminds readers that God does not overlook faithful work. Much of healthcare happens out of sight, the extra blanket at three in the morning, the patient explanation repeated for a frightened family, the careful charting no one praises. This verse says God remembers it. Hard work done in love is never wasted or forgotten, even when it goes unrecognized on the unit.

ReflectWhat unseen act of care from your recent work could you offer to God, trusting that he remembers it?

That is why we never give up. Though our bodies are dying, our spirits are being renewed every day.

2 Corinthians 4:16 · NLT

Paul wrote this after describing real hardship in his ministry, and he is honest that the body wears out. Anyone who has worked a twelve hour shift understands that. Yet he describes an inner renewal that continues day by day. For people who pour themselves out for others, this verse is an invitation to receive daily strength from God, through rest, prayer, and quiet moments with him.

ReflectWhat small rhythm, before or after a shift, could make space for God to renew your spirit?

A closer look

Going a little deeper.

Caring for the sick has deep roots in the Christian story. Jesus spent much of his ministry healing and touching people others avoided. In the early centuries, Christians became known for staying to care for the sick during epidemics when many fled. Later, hospitals, nursing orders, and modern nursing itself grew in part out of that tradition. Today's healthcare workers, whatever their role, carry forward a long line of people who saw care for the body as a way of loving God and neighbor.

Healthcare work can also be physically and emotionally heavy, and Scripture leaves room for that honesty. Jesus himself regularly withdrew to quiet places to pray and rest after long days among crowds. Many nurses and doctors find it helpful to keep a short verse on a badge card or in a locker, to pause for a breath and a brief prayer before entering a room, and to protect real rest on days off. Caring for yourself is part of being able to keep caring for others.

Ways to keep these verses close. Choose one verse to read at the start of a shift. Write a favorite on a card for your badge, locker, or nightstand. Share one with a coworker who might need it, along with a short personal note.

A prayer for nurses and healthcare workers

Lord, thank you for the calling to care for others. Give skill, clear thinking, and a gentle spirit for every patient and family served today. Let each person be seen as someone you love. When the shift is long, renew strength from the inside, and grant real rest. Thank you that you see every unseen act of care. Amen.

Questions

A few common questions.

What Bible verse is good for nurses?

Psalm 41:3, Matthew 25:40, and Luke 10:33 to 34 are dear to many nurses. They honor care for the sick as care Jesus takes personally. Isaiah 40:31 and Galatians 6:9 speak to long shifts.

What Bible verse talks about caring for the sick?

Matthew 25:40 says that care given to people in need is care given to Jesus. Luke 10:33 to 34 shows compassion as skilled, practical help: cleaning wounds, bandaging, and arranging ongoing care.

How can I pray for a nurse or healthcare worker?

Pray the prayer on this page. Ask God for skill, a gentle spirit, and rest after the shift. Hebrews 6:10 is a good verse to send in a note: God does not forget hard work done in love.
