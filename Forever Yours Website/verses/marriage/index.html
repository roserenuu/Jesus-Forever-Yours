Bible verses for building a marriage

These verses are for the years after the wedding: love that stays kind, unity that is practiced, and grace for hard days. For ceremony readings, cards, and toasts, see wedding scriptures.

This explains why a man leaves his father and mother and is joined to his wife, and the two are united into one.

Genesis 2:24

This verse describes marriage from the very beginning of Scripture. Two people leave, join, and become united. Jesus himself quoted this verse when he spoke about marriage.

ReflectHow might couples nurture the unity this verse describes in the everyday routines of marriage?

Two people are better off than one, for they can help each other succeed. If one person falls, the other can reach out and help. But someone who falls alone is in real trouble.

Ecclesiastes 4:9 to 10

Solomon observes that two are better than one. Partners help each other succeed and lift each other up. It is a practical picture of companionship.

ReflectWhat is one way you could help your spouse or a close friend succeed this week?

A person standing alone can be attacked and defeated, but two can stand back-to-back and conquer. Three are even better, for a triple-braided cord is not easily broken.

Ecclesiastes 4:12

Two people standing together are strong, and with God as the third strand, stronger still. In a marriage this is less a wedding image than a daily habit: praying together, forgiving quickly, and keeping God at the center when the week is ordinary or hard.

ReflectWhat one practice this week would keep God as the third strand in your marriage?

Always be humble and gentle. Be patient with each other, making allowance for each other's faults because of your love. Make every effort to keep yourselves united in the Spirit, binding yourselves together with peace.

Ephesians 4:2 to 3

Paul describes the qualities that keep relationships strong, humility, gentleness, and patience. Love makes allowance for faults. Unity is maintained through peace.

ReflectWhich quality, humility, gentleness, or patience, would most strengthen your closest relationships right now?

Above all, clothe yourselves with love, which binds us all together in perfect harmony.

Colossians 3:14

Paul describes love as the garment that holds every other virtue together. Clothing ourselves with love is a daily choice. It brings harmony to every relationship.

ReflectWhat would clothing yourself with love look like in one specific relationship this week?

Let no one split apart what God has joined together."

Mark 10:9

Jesus emphasizes that marriage is something God joins together. The covenant is not only spoken on a wedding day. It is guarded in ordinary choices: how we speak, how we forgive, and how we stay.

ReflectHow can you protect what God has joined together through one daily habit this week?

Most important of all, continue to show deep love for each other, for love covers a multitude of sins.

1 Peter 4:8

Peter calls deep love the most important thing. Love covers a multitude of sins, offering grace instead of keeping score. It is a practical word for marriage and friendship.

ReflectIs there a small offense you could choose to cover with love rather than keep score of?
A closer look

Love for the long years.

Scripture describes marriage as a covenant: a committed partnership built on love, faithfulness, and grace. These verses touch on unity, companionship, patience, and forgiveness, the qualities that strengthen a marriage over time. The cord of three strands is a picture for ordinary Tuesdays as much as for anniversaries. Couples sometimes choose one verse to read together each week, or pray one of these passages over each other.

Strong marriages are rarely built on grand gestures alone. They are built through thousands of small moments: a kind word, a shared prayer, a patient response, and a willingness to forgive. If you are looking for ceremony readings, cards, or a toast, those wedding scriptures live on our wedding scriptures page so the two lists do not compete.
