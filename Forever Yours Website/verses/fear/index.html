Bible verses About Fear

The phrase "do not be afraid" appears again and again in Scripture, usually paired with a reason. God is near. These verses are a reminder of that nearness.

2 Timothy 1:7Joshua 1:9Psalm 27:1Isaiah 43:1Psalm 56:3Deuteronomy 31:81 John 4:18Psalm 34:4

For God has not given us a spirit of fear and timidity, but of power, love, and self-discipline.

2 Timothy 1:7

Paul wrote this to Timothy, a young leader who may have felt timid in his role. He reminds him that God's Spirit brings power, love, and self-discipline. It is a balanced picture of courage that is both strong and kind.

ReflectWhich of the three gifts, power, love, or self-discipline, would you most like to grow in this season?

This is my command—be strong and courageous! Do not be afraid or discouraged. For the Lord your God is with you wherever you go.”

Joshua 1:9

God spoke these words to Joshua as he stepped into leadership after Moses. The command to be strong and courageous is grounded in a promise, God is with you wherever you go. Courage here is not the absence of challenge but confidence in God's presence.

ReflectWhere is wherever you go for you this week? Name the places and invite God's presence into each one.

The Lord is my light and my salvation— so why should I be afraid? The Lord is my fortress, protecting me from danger, so why should I tremble?

Psalm 27:1

David asks two questions that are really declarations of confidence. If God is light, salvation, and fortress, then fear loses its grip. It is a verse that turns attention from what might go wrong to who God is.

ReflectTry answering David's questions with your own words. Because God is my light, I do not need to fear what?

But now, O Jacob, listen to the Lord who created you. O Israel, the one who formed you says, “Do not be afraid, for I have ransomed you. I have called you by name; you are mine.

Isaiah 43:1

God speaks to his people as their Creator and Redeemer. He calls them by name and says you are mine. It is one of Scripture's most personal promises of belonging and care.

ReflectRead this verse with your own name in it. How does it feel to hear God say you are mine?

But when I am afraid, I will put my trust in you.

Psalm 56:3

This short verse is wonderfully honest. David does not claim never to feel afraid, he describes what he does when fear comes. Trust is a choice made in the moment, and this verse is an easy one to remember for those moments.

ReflectConsider memorizing this short verse so it is ready whenever you need a quick prayer of trust.

Do not be afraid or discouraged, for the Lord will personally go ahead of you. He will be with you; he will neither fail you nor abandon you.”

Deuteronomy 31:8

Moses encouraged Joshua with these words before Israel entered the promised land. God goes ahead, stays with, and never abandons his people. It is a promise that covers the road ahead and every step along the way.

ReflectHow does knowing God goes ahead of you, stays with you, and never leaves you cover every part of your journey?

Such love has no fear, because perfect love expels all fear. If we are afraid, it is for fear of punishment, and this shows that we have not fully experienced his perfect love.

1 John 4:18

John explains that perfect love drives out fear. The more we understand God's love, the less room fear has to take hold. It is an invitation to grow deeper in knowing how fully we are loved.

ReflectHow might growing in your understanding of God's love leave less room for fear in your daily life?

I prayed to the Lord, and he answered me. He freed me from all my fears.

Psalm 34:4

David shares a simple testimony, he prayed and God answered. The freedom he describes covers all his fears, not just some. It encourages readers to bring their own concerns to God in prayer.

ReflectWhat fear could you bring to God in prayer today, following David's simple example?
A closer look

Going a little deeper.

The phrase do not be afraid, or something close to it, appears many times across Scripture. It is spoken to shepherds, prophets, kings, and ordinary people. What is striking is that the command almost always comes with a reason, because God is present, because he goes ahead, because his love is perfect. Courage in the Bible is not pretending fear does not exist. It is choosing to trust God in the middle of it. Memorizing one short verse, such as Psalm 56 verse 3, can give you words to reach for whenever you need them.

It can also help to remember that many of the Bible's bravest people began afraid. Moses hesitated, Gideon asked for signs, and Esther knew the risk she faced. Their courage grew as they took the next step with God, and their stories show that faith and honest feelings can walk together.

Ways to keep these verses close. Choose one verse to read each morning this week and notice how it shapes your day. Write a favorite verse on a card for your desk, mirror, or nightstand. Share one verse with a friend or family member who might enjoy it, along with a short personal note.

A prayer

Lord, when fear comes, help me remember that you are with me. Replace fear with courage and confidence in your love. Amen.
