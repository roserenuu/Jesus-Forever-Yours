Bible verses for Caregivers

Caring for someone you love is one of the most generous things a person can do, and it often happens quietly, far from any spotlight. People look for these verses when they are helping an aging parent, supporting a spouse through illness, raising a child with special needs, or simply wanting to thank someone who gives that kind of care. Each passage below honors the work and points to the God who carries caregivers too.

Exodus 17:12John 19:26-27Psalm 68:191 Timothy 5:41 Peter 4:10

Moses’ arms soon became so tired he could no longer hold them up. So Aaron and Hur found a stone for him to sit on. Then they stood on each side of Moses, holding up his hands. So his hands held steady until sunset.

Exodus 17:12

During a long battle, Moses held up his staff while Israel fought, and his arms grew too tired to continue. Aaron and Hur did not take his place. They found him a seat and held up his hands until sunset. Caregiving often looks exactly like this. It is steady, practical help that lets someone keep going when their own strength runs out.

ReflectWho could you ask to help hold up your arms this week, or whose arms could you help hold up?

When Jesus saw his mother standing there beside the disciple he loved, he said to her, “Dear woman, here is your son.” And he said to this disciple, “Here is your mother.” And from then on this disciple took her into his home.

John 19:26-27

Even while suffering on the cross, Jesus made sure his mother would be looked after. He placed her in the care of a trusted disciple, who took her into his home from that day on. Few moments in the Gospels show so clearly how much Jesus values family care. Making a plan for someone you love is a holy act, not a small detail.

ReflectWhat step could you take to make sure someone you love feels cared for and secure?

Praise the Lord; praise God our savior! For each day he carries us in his arms.

Psalm 68:19

This line of praise contains one of the most tender images in the Psalms. God carries his people in his arms each day, not just on the hardest days. Caregivers spend so much time carrying others, sometimes literally. This verse reminds them that they are being carried as well, by a God who never tires.

ReflectWhere do you need to remember that God is carrying you today?

But if she has children or grandchildren, their first responsibility is to show godliness at home and repay their parents by taking care of them. This is something that pleases God.

1 Timothy 5:4

Paul wrote practical instructions for how the early church should care for widows, and he started with their own families. Caring for parents and grandparents is described as godliness at home and as something that pleases God. It can feel like a role no one notices. Scripture sees it and calls it good.

ReflectHow might it change your outlook to see your care as something that pleases God?

God has given each of you a gift from his great variety of spiritual gifts. Use them well to serve one another.

1 Peter 4:10

Peter reminds believers that each person has been given gifts meant for serving others. For a caregiver, those gifts might be patience, organization, a gentle voice, or the willingness to sit quietly by a bedside. None of them are small. The verse also invites friends and church members to use their own gifts to support the caregiver.

ReflectWhich of your gifts is showing up in the way you care for others right now?
A closer look

Going a little deeper.

November is recognized in the United States as National Family Caregivers Month, which makes it a natural time to share one of these verses with someone who is caring for a loved one. A short card with Exodus 17:12 and an offer to cover a few hours can mean more than any gift. Churches can use these passages for a caregiver appreciation Sunday, a support group, or a simple note tucked into a meal delivery.

Caregivers often put their own needs last, and long seasons of care can bring real exhaustion. Rest, friendship, and practical help are part of how God provides, so it is wise to accept offers of support and to look into local respite programs or caregiver groups. If you are caring for someone and feel overwhelmed, telling a trusted friend, pastor, or doctor is a sign of strength, not weakness.

Ways to keep these verses close. Choose one verse to read each morning this week and notice how it shapes your day. Write a favorite verse on a card for your desk, mirror, or nightstand. Share one verse with a friend or family member who might enjoy it, along with a short personal note.

A prayer

Lord, thank you for every person who cares for someone else with patience and love. Carry them in your arms today. Give them rest when they are tired, wisdom for each decision, and friends who will help hold up their hands. Let them know that their quiet work is seen and valued by you. Help me notice the caregivers around me and find real ways to support them. Amen.
