Bible verses for When You Are Tired and Weary

Everyone gets tired. Long workdays, busy seasons with family, caregiving, school, and the steady pace of modern life can leave both body and soul running low. The Bible speaks often and kindly about weariness, and it treats rest as a gift from God rather than a sign of weakness. These verses are a good place to pause, whether you are simply at the end of a full week or in a season that has asked a lot of you.

Isaiah 40:28Jeremiah 31:25Mark 6:31Psalm 116:7Hebrews 4:9-10

Have you never heard? Have you never understood? The Lord is the everlasting God, the Creator of all the earth. He never grows weak or weary. No one can measure the depths of his understanding.

Isaiah 40:28

Isaiah wrote to people who felt forgotten and worn out, and he begins by lifting their eyes to God. The Creator of all the earth never grows weak or weary. That matters because our limits are real, and his are not. The verses that follow promise strength to the weak, but the starting point is this: the one we lean on never runs out of energy, patience, or understanding.

ReflectWhich of your own limits could you hand over to the God who never grows weary?

For I have given rest to the weary and joy to the sorrowing.”

Jeremiah 31:25

Jeremiah prophesied during a time of loss and exile, yet this chapter is full of hope about restoration. Here God speaks in the first person, promising rest to the weary and joy to the sorrowing. The verse is short enough to memorize and carry through a demanding day. It frames rest not as something we must earn but as something God himself gives.

ReflectWhat kind of rest do you most need right now, physical, emotional, or spiritual?

Then Jesus said, “Let’s go off by ourselves to a quiet place and rest awhile.” He said this because there were so many people coming and going that Jesus and his apostles didn’t even have time to eat.

Mark 6:31

The disciples had just returned from a busy mission trip, eager to report everything, and the crowds were so constant they could not even eat. Jesus noticed and invited them away to rest. It is one of the most practical moments in the Gospels. Rest was not a reward for finishing everything. It was something Jesus prioritized for his friends when the work was still unfinished.

ReflectWhere could you plan a quiet place and a short time of rest in the coming week?

Let my soul be at rest again, for the Lord has been good to me.

Psalm 116:7

The writer of this psalm had been through a frightening season, and here he speaks to his own soul, telling it to return to rest. It is a helpful model for anyone whose mind keeps racing at the end of the day. Rest comes by remembering how good the Lord has been. Gratitude and rest are closely connected, and recalling God's past kindness can quiet a busy heart.

ReflectWhat is one way the Lord has been good to you that you could recall tonight?

So there is a special rest still waiting for the people of God. For all who have entered into God’s rest have rested from their labors, just as God did after creating the world.

Hebrews 4:9-10

The writer of Hebrews connects rest to the very first week of creation, when God rested after his work. He says a special rest still waits for God's people, a rest that comes from trusting God rather than striving to earn his favor. This rest is both a future hope and a present invitation. It gives permission to stop, breathe, and trust that God holds what we cannot finish.

ReflectWhat are you trying to carry or accomplish on your own that you could trust to God?
A closer look

Going a little deeper.

Scripture treats rest as part of God's good design. God rested on the seventh day, gave Israel a weekly Sabbath, and even built rest into the rhythm of farming. Elijah, one of the greatest prophets, was so exhausted at one point that he asked to die, and God's first response was not a lecture but sleep, food, and more sleep. Being tired is not a spiritual failure. It is a human reality God meets with care.

Small rhythms of rest can make a real difference. Some people set aside one evening a week with no screens, others take a short walk outside at lunch, and others end each day by reading one verse and naming one thing they are grateful for. If weariness lasts for a long time or comes with other symptoms, it is wise to talk with a doctor or trusted counselor as well. Caring for your body is part of caring for the life God has given you.

Ways to keep these verses close. Choose one verse to read each morning this week and notice how it shapes your day. Write a favorite verse on a card for your desk, mirror, or nightstand. Share one verse with a friend or family member who might enjoy it, along with a short personal note.

A prayer

Lord, you never grow weak or weary, but I do. Thank you that you understand my limits and invite me to rest. Quiet my mind, refresh my body, and restore my soul. Help me remember how good you have been to me, and teach me to trust you with what I cannot finish. Give me rest tonight, and strength for tomorrow. Amen.
