Bible verses About Peace

The peace Scripture describes is deeper than a quiet moment. It is a settled heart that comes from knowing God. These verses are a good place to begin or end the day.

John 14:27Isaiah 26:3Philippians 4:7Colossians 3:15Numbers 6:24-26Romans 15:132 Thessalonians 3:16

“I am leaving you with a gift—peace of mind and heart. And the peace I give is a gift the world cannot give. So don’t be troubled or afraid.

John 14:27

Jesus spoke these words to his friends on the night before the cross, a time when they had every reason to be unsettled. He describes his peace as a gift, something given rather than earned, and different in kind from the calm the world offers. It does not depend on circumstances, which is why it can hold steady when circumstances shift.

ReflectHow is the peace Jesus describes different from the temporary calm the world offers, and where could you make room to receive it today?

You will keep in perfect peace all who trust in you, all whose thoughts are fixed on you!

Isaiah 26:3

Isaiah describes peace that is perfect, complete and whole. It belongs to those whose thoughts are fixed on God. The verse suggests that where we rest our attention shapes the peace we experience.

ReflectWhat helps you keep your thoughts fixed on God throughout a busy day, a verse, a song, or a short prayer?

Then you will experience God’s peace, which exceeds anything we can understand. His peace will guard your hearts and minds as you live in Christ Jesus.

Philippians 4:7

Paul describes God's peace as beyond understanding, a calm that does not depend on having every answer. It stands guard over hearts and minds like a protective watch. That peace is found in Christ Jesus, close to him.

ReflectPicture God's peace standing guard over your heart and mind. What might it be guarding you from today?

And let the peace that comes from Christ rule in your hearts. For as members of one body you are called to live in peace. And always be thankful.

Colossians 3:15

Paul invites Christ's peace to rule in our hearts, like an umpire settling questions. That peace is meant to be shared within community. The verse ends with a simple call to be thankful, a habit that nurtures peace.

ReflectHow might letting Christ's peace act as the referee in your relationships change a conversation this week?

‘May the Lord bless you and protect you. May the Lord smile on you and be gracious to you. May the Lord show you his favor and give you his peace.’

Numbers 6:24-26

This is the priestly blessing God gave Aaron to speak over Israel. Each line builds on the last, blessing, protection, favor, grace, and peace. It has been spoken over congregations, families, and children for more than three thousand years.

ReflectConsider speaking this blessing over your family, a friend, or your home before bed tonight.

I pray that God, the source of hope, will fill you completely with joy and peace because you trust in him. Then you will overflow with confident hope through the power of the Holy Spirit.

Romans 15:13

Paul calls God the source of hope, the place where hope begins. His prayer is generous, asking that readers be filled completely and then overflow. Joy and peace come through trusting, and the Holy Spirit is the power behind it all.

ReflectRead Paul's prayer aloud over yourself or a loved one, and notice the progression from trust to joy and peace to overflowing hope.

Now may the Lord of peace himself give you his peace at all times and in every situation. The Lord be with you all.

2 Thessalonians 3:16

Paul closes his letter with a prayer for peace at all times and in every situation. He calls God the Lord of peace himself. It is a generous blessing that covers every part of life.

ReflectWhat does peace at all times and in every situation look like in the specific situations you are facing this week?
A closer look

Going a little deeper.

Biblical peace, often connected to the Hebrew word shalom, means far more than the absence of conflict. It describes wholeness, well-being, and right relationship with God and others. These verses show that peace is both a gift from God and something we are invited to practice. It guards our hearts, rules in our relationships, and grows as our thoughts are fixed on God. The ancient blessing from Numbers is a beautiful way to speak peace over family members, friends, or a home. Many families read it together at the end of a meal or before bed.

Jesus is called the Prince of Peace in Isaiah 9, and his first words to his disciples after the resurrection were peace be with you. Peace is at the very heart of the good news. Making space for it might look like a short pause before a meal, a slower morning, or a few deep breaths while repeating a verse from this page.

Ways to keep these verses close. Choose one verse to read each morning this week and notice how it shapes your day. Write a favorite verse on a card for your desk, mirror, or nightstand. Share one verse with a friend or family member who might enjoy it, along with a short personal note.

A prayer

Prince of Peace, quiet my heart and keep my thoughts fixed on you. Let your peace rule in my home and in my relationships today. Amen.
