Bible verses About Grief and Loss

Grief is one of the most human experiences there is, and Scripture meets it with tenderness. These verses hold both honest sorrow and lasting hope.

Psalm 34:18Matthew 5:4Revelation 21:42 Corinthians 1:3-4John 11:25Psalm 147:31 Thessalonians 4:13-14Isaiah 41:10

The Lord is close to the brokenhearted; he rescues those whose spirits are crushed.

Psalm 34:18

David wrote this psalm after a difficult season of his own life. He describes God as close, not distant, to those whose hearts are broken. It is one of the most tender pictures of God's nearness in the whole Bible.

ReflectHow does it change your picture of God to know he draws especially close to the brokenhearted rather than stepping away?

God blesses those who mourn, for they will be comforted.

Matthew 5:4

This is one of the Beatitudes from the Sermon on the Mount. Jesus calls those who mourn blessed, not because sorrow is good but because comfort is promised. It honors grief as something God takes seriously and responds to with care.

ReflectWho in your life is mourning right now, and how might you be part of the comfort Jesus promises them?

He will wipe every tear from their eyes, and there will be no more death or sorrow or crying or pain. All these things are gone forever.”

Revelation 21:4

This verse describes the new creation at the end of the Bible's story. God himself wipes away every tear, a remarkably personal image. It offers a lasting hope that sorrow and pain will one day be gone forever.

ReflectPicture God personally wiping away tears. How does this future hope shape the way you carry sorrow today?

All praise to God, the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ. God is our merciful Father and the source of all comfort. He comforts us in all our troubles so that we can comfort others. When they are troubled, we will be able to give them the same comfort God has given us.

2 Corinthians 1:3-4

Paul calls God the source of all comfort. The comfort God gives does not stop with one person but flows outward to others. It shows how experiences of God's care can become a gift to share with people around us.

ReflectThink of a time you received comfort. How might that experience equip you to comfort someone else this week?

Jesus told her, “I am the resurrection and the life. Anyone who believes in me will live, even after dying.

John 11:25

Jesus spoke these words to Martha shortly before raising her brother Lazarus. He does not simply promise resurrection someday, he says I am the resurrection. It is one of the clearest statements of Christian hope beyond death.

ReflectJesus said I am the resurrection rather than I will bring resurrection. What does it mean that hope is found in a person?

He heals the brokenhearted and bandages their wounds.

Psalm 147:3

This verse sits in a psalm that also describes God counting the stars and calling them by name. The same God who arranges the heavens bends low to bandage wounded hearts. The picture is tender and personal, care that is attentive to the smallest hurt.

ReflectHow does it change your view of God to know that the One who names the stars also pays close attention to every wounded heart?

And now, dear brothers and sisters, we want you to know what will happen to the believers who have died so you will not grieve like people who have no hope. For since we believe that Jesus died and was raised to life again, we also believe that when Jesus returns, God will bring back with him the believers who have died.

1 Thessalonians 4:13-14

Paul writes to a church that had questions about loved ones who had died. He does not tell them not to grieve, but to grieve with hope. That hope rests on the resurrection of Jesus and the promise of reunion.

ReflectHow can grief and hope exist side by side? Consider writing down one memory and one hope together.

Don’t be afraid, for I am with you. Don’t be discouraged, for I am your God. I will strengthen you and help you. I will hold you up with my victorious right hand.

Isaiah 41:10

This verse stacks one promise on another. God is present, God is yours, God will strengthen, help, and hold you up. The image of a victorious right hand comes from the ancient picture of a king's strong arm, the hand that protects and leads. It is a verse many people memorize because every line offers something steady to stand on.

ReflectWhich of the five promises in this verse speaks most clearly to your day, presence, belonging, strength, help, or being held up?
A closer look

Going a little deeper.

Scripture never rushes grief. The psalms give space to sorrow, Jesus wept at the tomb of his friend Lazarus, and Paul encouraged believers to grieve with hope rather than not to grieve at all. These verses hold both truths, that loss is real and that God's comfort is real too. Grief often comes in waves, and there is no correct timeline for it. Reading a verse a day, writing out a memory alongside it, or sharing one of these passages with someone else who is grieving can be small, meaningful ways to carry both sorrow and hope together.

If you are walking alongside someone who is grieving, simple presence often matters more than finding the perfect words. Sending a verse, bringing a meal, or remembering an anniversary can communicate care. Many churches also offer grief support groups, which can be a helpful place to share stories and find companionship with others who understand.

Ways to keep these verses close. Choose one verse to read each morning this week and notice how it shapes your day. Write a favorite verse on a card for your desk, mirror, or nightstand. Share one verse with a friend or family member who might enjoy it, along with a short personal note.

A prayer

Lord, you are close to the brokenhearted. Hold me and everyone who is grieving today. Comfort us, and keep the hope of your promises in front of us. Amen.
