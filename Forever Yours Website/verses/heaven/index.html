Bible verses About Heaven

Heaven is one of the most hopeful themes in the Bible, and it is also one of the most wondered about. People look up these verses after a loss, before a funeral, while teaching children, or simply because they want to know what God has promised. The passages below describe heaven less as a distant idea and more as a home, prepared by Someone who loves us and wants us near.

John 14:2-3Philippians 3:201 Corinthians 2:92 Corinthians 5:1Revelation 21:3

There is more than enough room in my Father’s home. If this were not so, would I have told you that I am going to prepare a place for you? When everything is ready, I will come and get you, so that you will always be with me where I am.

John 14:2-3

Jesus spoke these words at the Last Supper, on the night before the cross, when his friends were confused and afraid. He did not give them a map or a timeline. He gave them a promise about a home with plenty of room and a personal return. The heart of heaven in this passage is not the place itself but being with Jesus, always, where he is.

But we are citizens of heaven, where the Lord Jesus Christ lives. And we are eagerly waiting for him to return as our Savior.

Philippians 3:20

Paul wrote to believers in Philippi, a city proud of its Roman citizenship and its privileges. He reminds them that their deepest citizenship belongs somewhere else. That does not make life here unimportant. Citizens of heaven still love their neighbors and work faithfully. It simply means their hope is anchored in a King who is coming, not in any earthly status.

ReflectHow might seeing yourself as a citizen of heaven change the way you approach an ordinary day?

That is what the Scriptures mean when they say, “No eye has seen, no ear has heard, and no mind has imagined what God has prepared for those who love him.”

1 Corinthians 2:9

Paul borrows language from Isaiah to describe the wisdom and goodness God has prepared. Many readers apply it to heaven, and it fits, because the verse admits the limits of our imagination. Our best pictures of heaven fall short. Whatever we can dream up, God's plans are richer still, and they are prepared for those who love him.

ReflectWhat is one good gift in this life that helps you imagine how much more God has prepared?

For we know that when this earthly tent we live in is taken down (that is, when we die and leave this earthly body), we will have a house in heaven, an eternal body made for us by God himself and not by human hands.

2 Corinthians 5:1

Paul was a tentmaker by trade, so the picture of a tent being taken down came naturally to him. A tent is useful but temporary, worn by weather and travel. He contrasts it with a house built by God that will last. The verse speaks honestly about death while placing it inside a larger promise of a body and home that will never wear out.

ReflectHow does the picture of a temporary tent and a lasting house shape the way you think about life and loss?

I heard a loud shout from the throne, saying, “Look, God’s home is now among his people! He will live with them, and they will be his people. God himself will be with them.

Revelation 21:3

Near the very end of the Bible, John hears an announcement that sums up the whole story. From the garden of Eden onward, God has wanted to live among his people. Here that desire is fully realized. Heaven is described not as people escaping to God but as God making his home with them, close, present, and permanent.

ReflectWhat feelings come up when you picture God making his home among his people?
A closer look

Going a little deeper.

The Bible uses many images for heaven, including a city, a wedding feast, a garden with a river and the tree of life, and a home with many rooms. These pictures are not meant to be read like a floor plan. Together they point to a life of beauty, belonging, celebration, and rest, with nothing broken and no one left out who has come to God through Jesus. Scripture also speaks of a new heaven and a new earth, which suggests that God's plan is to renew creation rather than discard it.

For many families, verses about heaven become most meaningful around a funeral or memorial. They can be read aloud at a service, written in a sympathy card, or printed on a small memorial card. They are also helpful when children ask where a grandparent has gone. A simple answer drawn from John 14, that Jesus prepares a place and brings his people home, is often enough to bring comfort and open a conversation.

Ways to keep these verses close. Choose one verse to read each morning this week and notice how it shapes your day. Write a favorite verse on a card for your desk, mirror, or nightstand. Share one verse with a friend or family member who might enjoy it, along with a short personal note.

A prayer

Father, thank you for the hope of heaven and for the promise that Jesus is preparing a place for those who love him. When I think about loss or about my own future, help me rest in that promise. Teach me to live today as a citizen of heaven, with love for the people around me and joy in your presence. Thank you that one day you will make your home with your people forever. Amen.
