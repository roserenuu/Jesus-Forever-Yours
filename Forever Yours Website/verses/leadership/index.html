Bible verses About Leadership

Leadership shows up in many places, from managing a team or running a business to leading a classroom, a ministry, a household, or a group of friends. The Bible has a great deal to say about it, and much of it turns common ideas upside down. These verses describe leaders who serve, listen, stay humble, and lead by example, which makes them useful for anyone with influence, whatever their title.

Mark 10:45Philippians 2:3-4Proverbs 11:14Exodus 18:211 Peter 5:2-3

For even the Son of Man came not to be served but to serve others and to give his life as a ransom for many.”

Mark 10:45

Jesus spoke these words after two of his disciples asked for the most important seats in his kingdom. Instead of rebuking ambition outright, he redefined greatness. The greatest leader in history described his mission as serving and giving his life for others. For anyone leading today, this verse sets the standard. Leadership is measured by how well we serve the people entrusted to us.

ReflectWho are the people you lead or influence, and what is one way you could serve them this week?

Don’t be selfish; don’t try to impress others. Be humble, thinking of others as better than yourselves. Don’t look out only for your own interests, but take an interest in others, too.

Philippians 2:3-4

Paul writes this just before a beautiful passage about Jesus humbling himself. Being humble here does not mean thinking poorly of yourself. It means paying attention to others and valuing their needs. For a leader, that looks like listening before deciding, sharing credit, and noticing the person who is struggling. These are small habits that build deep trust.

ReflectWhich person on your team, in your home, or in your circle could use more of your attention right now?

Without wise leadership, a nation falls; there is safety in having many advisers.

Proverbs 11:14

This proverb speaks to nations, but its wisdom applies to any team or family. Good leaders do not carry every decision alone. They gather wise people around them and listen to different viewpoints. Seeking counsel is not a sign of weakness. According to this verse, it is a source of safety and a mark of wisdom.

ReflectWho are the wise advisers you turn to, and is there a decision you could bring to them?

But select from all the people some capable, honest men who fear God and hate bribes. Appoint them as leaders over groups of one thousand, one hundred, fifty, and ten.

Exodus 18:21

Moses was worn out from settling every dispute by himself, and Jethro, the father of his wife Zipporah, offered practical advice. Share the load, and choose leaders carefully. The qualities named are capability, honesty, reverence for God, and a refusal to be bought. Thousands of years later, these remain a strong checklist for choosing leaders and for growing as one.

ReflectWhich of the qualities in this verse would you most like to grow in as a leader?

Care for the flock that God has entrusted to you. Watch over it willingly, not grudgingly—not for what you will get out of it, but because you are eager to serve God. Don’t lord it over the people assigned to your care, but lead them by your own good example.

1 Peter 5:2-3

Peter writes to church elders, using the image of a shepherd caring for a flock. He warns against three temptations, leading reluctantly, leading for personal gain, and leading by domination. In their place he describes leaders who serve eagerly and set a good example. The image of a shepherd suggests closeness, patience, and protection.

ReflectWhat does leading by your own good example look like in the place where you lead most often?
A closer look

Going a little deeper.

Scripture includes many leaders, and it is honest about their strengths and failures. Nehemiah prayed before he planned and then organized a whole city to rebuild its walls. Deborah led Israel with wisdom and courage. David was a gifted king who also made serious mistakes and needed correction. Jesus washed his disciples' feet, a task usually given to the lowest servant, and told them to follow his example. Together these stories show that godly leadership combines vision with humility and accountability.

Leaders can put these verses into practice through simple routines. Some begin a weekly meeting by asking how each person is doing before turning to the agenda. Others keep a short list of trusted advisers to call before big decisions. Writing Mark 10:45 on a card near a desk can be a daily reminder of the purpose of leadership. Parents can apply the same principles at home by listening carefully, admitting mistakes, and modeling the character they hope to see.

Ways to keep these verses close. Choose one verse to read each morning this week and notice how it shapes your day. Write a favorite verse on a card for your desk, mirror, or nightstand. Share one verse with a friend or family member who might enjoy it, along with a short personal note.

A prayer

Lord Jesus, thank you for showing what true leadership looks like by coming to serve. Help me lead the people around me with humility, honesty, and care. Give me wisdom to listen, courage to make good decisions, and grace to admit when I am wrong. Keep me from selfish ambition, and help me set an example that points others to you. Amen.
