Bible verses About Kindness

Kindness may be one of the most practical ways to live out faith every day. The Bible describes it as part of God's own character and as something his Spirit grows in people. These verses show what kindness looks like, why it matters, and how it can shape ordinary conversations, homes, workplaces, and friendships.

Colossians 3:12Proverbs 3:3Galatians 5:22-23Luke 6:35Romans 2:4

Since God chose you to be the holy people he loves, you must clothe yourselves with tenderhearted mercy, kindness, humility, gentleness, and patience.

Colossians 3:12

Paul uses the image of getting dressed. Just as people choose clothes each morning, believers can choose to put on mercy, kindness, humility, gentleness, and patience. Notice the order of the verse. Before the instruction comes the identity, chosen, holy, and loved by God. Kindness flows from knowing we are already loved, not from trying to earn that love.

ReflectWhich of the qualities in this verse would you like to put on intentionally tomorrow morning?

Never let loyalty and kindness leave you! Tie them around your neck as a reminder. Write them deep within your heart.

Proverbs 3:3

This proverb uses two memorable images, tying loyalty and kindness around the neck like a necklace and writing them on the heart. One is visible to others and the other is deep inside. Together they suggest that kindness should be both seen in how we act and rooted in who we are. The Hebrew word behind kindness here often describes God's own steadfast, faithful love.

But the Holy Spirit produces this kind of fruit in our lives: love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, and self-control. There is no law against these things!

Galatians 5:22-23

Paul calls these qualities fruit, which is a helpful picture. Fruit is not manufactured overnight. It grows slowly as a healthy tree stays rooted and receives what it needs. Kindness sits in the middle of the list, next to patience and goodness. As people stay close to God, his Spirit gradually grows these qualities in them, often in ways others notice first.

ReflectWhere have you seen kindness grow in your life over time, perhaps more than you expected?

“Love your enemies! Do good to them. Lend to them without expecting to be repaid. Then your reward from heaven will be very great, and you will truly be acting as children of the Most High, for he is kind to those who are unthankful and wicked.

Luke 6:35

Jesus sets a remarkably high bar here. Kindness toward friends comes naturally, but he calls his followers to do good even to people who are difficult or unappreciative. He grounds this in God's own character, since God is kind even to the unthankful. This kind of kindness is not weakness or approval of wrongdoing. It is a choice to reflect the generosity of the Father.

ReflectIs there someone who is hard to be kind to, and what is one small good thing you could do for them?

Don’t you see how wonderfully kind, tolerant, and patient God is with you? Does this mean nothing to you? Can’t you see that his kindness is intended to turn you from your sin?

Romans 2:4

Paul reminds readers that God's kindness has a purpose. His patience is not indifference. It is an open door, an invitation to turn toward him. Many people imagine that God changes hearts mainly through fear, but this verse says his kindness leads the way. It is a helpful reminder for anyone hoping to influence others, too, since kindness often opens hearts that pressure cannot.

ReflectHow has God's kindness drawn you closer to him at some point in your life?
A closer look

Going a little deeper.

The Bible's stories are full of kindness in action. Ruth gleaned in the fields to provide for her mother-in-law, and Boaz quietly left extra grain for her. David showed kindness to Mephibosheth, the son of his friend Jonathan, by inviting him to eat at the king's table. Jesus noticed people others ignored, a woman at a well, a tax collector in a tree, children brought to him for a blessing. In each case, kindness meant paying attention to someone and then doing something concrete for them.

Kindness often shows up in small, practical habits. Learning and using someone's name, writing a short note of thanks, letting someone go ahead in line, checking in on a neighbor, or speaking gently in a tense moment are all simple ways to live these verses. Some families or small groups choose one act of kindness a day for a week and share what they did and what they noticed. Over time, these small acts can shape both the giver and the people around them.

Ways to keep these verses close. Choose one verse to read each morning this week and notice how it shapes your day. Write a favorite verse on a card for your desk, mirror, or nightstand. Share one verse with a friend or family member who might enjoy it, along with a short personal note.

A prayer

Lord, thank you for your kindness to me, which is patient, generous, and full of grace. Help me clothe myself in kindness each day. Grow the fruit of your Spirit in me, and give me eyes to notice people who need a kind word or a helping hand. Help me be kind even when it is hard, and let my kindness point others to you. Amen.
